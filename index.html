THE IDEA

Keep the remainder.
Remember the shift.

A fragment’s summary stores two polynomials: its remainder starting from zero, and the effect of shifting by its length. When we join fragments, we shift the left remainder by the right length, then add the right remainder.

Composition is associative. We can change the shape of the computation without changing the answer—and retain intermediate summaries for the next edit.

combine (Summary p m) (Summary q n) =
  Summary (multiply p n `xor` q) (multiply m n)

This pilot uses CRC-32/ISO-HDLC: reflected polynomial 0xedb88320, initial and final XOR 0xffffffff. In the reflected polynomial representation, 0x80000000 represents one.
